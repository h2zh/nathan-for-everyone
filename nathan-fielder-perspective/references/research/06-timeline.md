## Summary

- Born 12 May 1983 in Vancouver to social-worker parents; business degree (BCom, UVic, 2005); brief brokerage job; moved to Toronto c. 2006 for comedy (Humber, Laugh Sabbath) [secondary, S1, S2].
- Apprenticeship in Canadian TV: Canadian Idol (segment producer/interviewer), then CBC's This Hour Has 22 Minutes with the "Nathan On Your Side" consumer-report segment, which became the seed of Nathan for You [secondary, S1, S2].
- Nathan for You (Comedy Central, 2013-2017, 4 seasons, 32 episodes) is business-advice parody built on staged interventions. Dumb Starbucks (Feb 2014), Summit Ice (2015), and the finale "Finding Frances" (2017) mark its drift from gag to sincere human drama [secondary, S1, S3].
- HBO era: overall deal (Aug 2019); How To with John Wilson (EP, 2020-2023); The Rehearsal S1 (Jul-Aug 2022), The Curse (Nov 2023-Jan 2024, co-created with Benny Safdie), The Rehearsal S2 (Apr-May 2025, aviation) [secondary, S1, S3, S4, S5].
- 2026: feature directorial debut, You Can See Everything (with Lance Oppenheim, A24, about Elizabeth Holmes). Surprise Telluride premiere 6 Sep, US release 16 Oct, a record documentary opening, and a live Oscar campaign. The Rehearsal S3 is NOT confirmed [secondary, S6-S12].
- Pattern [inference]: the work moves from "exploit a rule or loophole for a business" to "rehearse a human situation to reduce risk and fear", then to "document a person you cannot control while you are inside the frame."

## Turning points in his thinking

1. Nathan On Your Side to Nathan for You (2008-2013): the consumer-reporter conceit gets turned into a method: propose an absurd but internally logical business fix, then stage it with real people. [secondary, S1, S2] [inference: logic over social comfort, with real people as co-participants.]
2. Dumb Starbucks (Feb 2014): the loophole becomes the joke, and the team operates in a legal grey area, with the reveal itself a staged event. [secondary, S1, S3]
3. Seasons 2-3 (2014-2015): critics noted the show gaining "heart"; Summit Ice entangled a real cause with a gag, and Fielder later called it his "proudest achievement" (as quoted in Wikipedia's account). [secondary, S1, S3]
4. Finding Frances (Nov 2017): the finale shifts from business to love, loss, and a real woman's story. Errol Morris called it "unfathomably great." [secondary, S3] [inference: the first clear step from commerce toward empathy and the ethics of intervening in a real person's life.]
5. The Rehearsal S1 (2022): the rehearsal technique (formerly Nathan for You's behind-the-scenes role-play, per one commentator) becomes the product itself. Critic Richard Brody called it cruel and arrogant in 2022 [secondary, S4]; others read it as empathy about manipulation and responsibility [secondary, S17].
6. The Rehearsal S2 (2025): preparation applied to safety: pilots who rehearse speaking up and taking control. The origin is John Goglia's proposal for role-play simulation [secondary, S17]. [inference: from personal anxiety to institutional safety; here the work has measurable policy contact, see below.]
7. The Curse (2023): fictionalized self-critique about performed goodness [inference from S5's premise only; I did not read analysis of its themes].
8. You Can See Everything (2026): Fielder abandons a controlled set and enters a real person's home, appears on camera, and reportedly negotiated a "kill switch" with A24 because he doubted he could find a distinctive angle [secondary, S9]. [inference: the Rehearsal question "how much can you control" inverted into "what happens when you can't."] The Hollywood Reporter says he dispenses with the pretense of "objectivity" because he is on camera [secondary, S10].
