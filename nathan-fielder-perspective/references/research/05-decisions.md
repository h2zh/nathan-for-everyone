# 05 - Decisions, stunts and actions (Nathan Fielder)

Research date: 2026-11-04. Tags: [primary] = Fielder's own words/on-screen acts as reported directly (interview, on-air, the work itself); [secondary] = press/reference reporting; [inference] = my synthesis. Note: WebFetch returns model-compressed page summaries, so quotes below were taken from those and are flagged where wording is not independently verified. Very few direct Fielder explanations of *why* he ended Nathan for You exist; that gap is real.

## Summary

- Fielder's working method, across Nathan for You (2013-17), The Rehearsal (2022, 2025) and the 2026 film *You Can See Everything*, is to treat a real-world rule, policy or social convention as a puzzle, find a technically-legal reading nobody has used, and then follow it much further than a sane person would. The comedy and the feeling arrive when real people react to the commitment. [inference, supported by the evidence below]
- His decisions are consistently "yes, and, and, and": each escalation is justified by the previous step's logic, not by whether it is advisable. [inference]
- Career moves are mostly cautious on paper (business degree, brokerage job, then comedy; Nathan for You ended on a high note by his choice) but the work itself becomes steadily more personal and risky (S1 child actors, S2 real pilot licence, Holmes film where he moves in). [secondary + inference]
- Fallout response pattern: he does not apologise in the abstract; he stays in persona, doubles down in public (FAA "They're dumb", Paramount+ "Germany" bit), and routes self-reflection into the next piece of work rather than into statements. Exceptions exist (S1 finale shows him questioning himself on screen). [secondary]

## Plan-building pattern (the repeatable recipe)

1. **Start from a real constraint, rule or incentive.** Trademark law, indoor smoking bans, price-match policies, rebate terms, pilot-hour minimums, passenger-carrying rules. [secondary]
2. **Find a literal reading that is technically permitted.** Parody defence (Dumb Starbucks); "theatrical performance" exemption (Smokers Allowed, per episode summaries); filling a 737 with non-paying actors so no passenger rule is broken and the usual 1,500-hour airline hiring requirement does not apply (Rehearsal S2). Fielder's team used lawyers/researchers on some segments [secondary, low-quality sources; treat as weak]. For S2, former NTSB member John Goglia confirmed the route and said there was a loophole [secondary, EW via AOL].
3. **Do it as a real business/real event, not a sketch.** Per the Nathan for You Wikipedia summary, businesses were real and consented to filming but employees did not know it was a comedy; each 22-min episode took about 90 hours of footage; Fielder called it "a very inefficient way of making TV." [secondary]
4. **Commit absurdly far past normal.** A fake Starbucks with full branding and an announced Brooklyn branch; a 17-hour mountain hike for a gas-rebate; two years of pilot training; buying/using a real 737. [secondary]
5. **Leave the deadpan persona in place and let humans react.** The persona is built on his own social anxiety and was not meant to "feel like a comedy character" [secondary, Wikipedia paraphrase of Fielder]. Genuine emotion leaks in when someone (Bill Heath, Remy, a co-pilot) stops following the script.
6. **Use rehearsal/prediction as a planning tool.** The Rehearsal grew from Nathan for You, where role-played run-throughs of stunts predicted participant reactions, often wrongly; Fielder found the urge to control the future funny and made it the premise. [secondary, Wikipedia]
7. **When the plan fails, the failure is the content.** Electronics-store plan collapsed; Dumb Starbucks closed by health department; S2 FAA rejection. The next step is to film or comment on the collapse. [secondary + inference]

## Stunts and plans

Format: context -> logic -> what happened -> consequences -> later reflection -> words vs actions.

### Dumb Starbucks (S2E5, aired 29 Jul 2014; shop opened 7 Feb 2014)
- Context/logic: a struggling LA coffee shop (Los Feliz) is rebranded as a Starbucks "parody"; claim: adding "Dumb" meets minimum requirements for parody, so name/logo are legal; supporting document even asserted the shop was an art gallery and coffee was art. [secondary: Mediaite, NBC, CSM]
- What happened: opened Friday 7 Feb, free "dumb" drinks, long lines, press conference on Monday 10 Feb revealing Fielder; he said he would keep it open "forever" and open Brooklyn next. LA County health department shut it that day for no permit. Starbucks denied affiliation. [secondary: NBC]
- Consequences: legal theory never tested in court. A Notre Dame professor said trademark law lacks a definitive parody defence. Episode shows closure and customers directed to the owner's real cafe. [secondary]
- Later reflection: on Kimmel he discussed ups and downs of the stunt [secondary, headline only; no detail retrieved].
- Say/do: stated aim in public was to get rich as a new owner (in-character); actual outcome was publicity for the owner's cafe. [secondary + inference]

### Summit Ice (S3E2, 22 Oct 2015)
- Logic: after a jacket maker published a tribute to a Holocaust denier, Nathan launches his own Holocaust-awareness apparel brand as a business response. [secondary: Wikipedia episode list]
- Result: brand reportedly raised over $150,000 by 2017 (Wikipedia); Fielder claims millions (self-reported, per Jewish News/other; UK paper cites a lower figure of more than GBP 240,000 - sources differ). [secondary]
- Fallout: Paramount+ removed the episode (first reported as Germany division, then other regions) after Oct 2023-era concerns; Paramount said it was "following a standards review." Fielder responded inside Rehearsal S2 by likening Paramount+ Germany to Nazis, and called Summit Ice his "proudest achievement" (also repeated on CNN). Date of removal discovery conflicts between sources (late 2022 vs late 2023). [primary on-air + secondary]
- Say/do: he turned a business gag into a real charitable stream and then used his newer platform to retaliate against the removal; consistent with the "commit past normal" rule, but the retaliation was a satirical attack on a corporate partner, not an appeal. [inference]

### Smokers Allowed (S3E5, 12 Nov 2015)
- Logic: indoor smoking bans exempt theatre performances; Nathan frames bar smoking as a "play" for an audience of two. [secondary: Wikipedia list; other claims about "loophole" language come from weaker sources]
- Result: the audience enjoyed it, so he staged a real production with hired actors. The owner disapproved, so Nathan had the actress say she loved it. [secondary]
- Pattern: exploit an exception literally, then build the real thing to make the exception honest.

### Gas-station rebate on a mountain (S1E4, 21 Mar 2013)
- Logic: a rebate that sounds generous but requires hiking, riddles and an overnight camp, so few collect. [secondary]
- Result: after 17 hours Nathan told the three remaining customers there was no rebate; they left unaware he had lied. [secondary]
- Note: shows early willingness to deceive participants for a joke; no later reflection found.

### The Movement (S3E3, 29 Oct 2015)
- Logic: invent a fitness fad to get a moving company free labour from enthusiasts; a ghost-written book reached Amazon's bestseller list. [secondary]
- What happened: Nathan found the bodybuilder still used his regular gym, confronted him, then recruited movers. [secondary]

### Claw of Shame (S1E7, 11 Apr 2013)
- Revisited failed first-season ideas; staged handcuff escape just before a robot claw pulled his pants down in front of children, with a police officer present. [secondary]
- Logic: he subjects himself to the humiliation instead of a participant. [inference]

### Finding Frances (S4E7, 9 Nov 2017)
- Context: Bill Heath, Season 2 Bill Gates impersonator, wants to find a lover from decades ago; only a first name. [secondary: The Ringer, Forward]
- Logic: Nathan applies the usual schemes - posing as producers of a *Mud* sequel to get a yearbook, staging a reunion to find Frances. [secondary]
- What happened: Bill becomes obsessive; Nathan stages role-play with an actress to teach respect; Bill gropes her thigh; Nathan hires an escort (Maci, $350/hr), Bill refuses, Nathan ends up spending time with her and the finale is ambiguous about whether the warmth is real or staged. [secondary: The Ringer]
- Consequences: critically acclaimed (Errol Morris praised it; Comedy Central said the show ended in Oct 2018 with Fielder choosing to focus on other projects). [secondary]
- Say/do: stated plan (reunite Bill with Frances) quickly gave way to Nathan's own emotional storyline; a clear case where the plan's real subject changed mid-flight. [inference]

### Electronics-store / price-match plan (S3E1, 15 Oct 2015)
- $1 TVs advertised to exploit Best Buy price-matching; Best Buy refused; Nathan escalated (fake insanity defence, planted witness), witness refused, he dropped the case and introduced a different applicant to the store owner. [secondary]

### Souvenir shop/film set and hot-dog stand (S2E2, S2E7)
- Souvenir shop turned into a "film set" so tourists would act as extras; he ended up having to make a film (The Web) and created a fake festival to credit it. Hot-dog stand let people skip lines for a valid reason; a liar was tricked into a "lobster meal at sea" and punished with shocking gum. [secondary]
- Both show: one lie requires a bigger structure to keep it true.

### Pizzeria 8-minute guarantee (S1E1, 28 Feb 2013)
- Free pizza if not delivered in 8 minutes - but the free pizza was an inch in diameter. Textbook "literal compliance, zero spirit" move. [secondary]

### Petting Zoo Hero hoax (S1E2)
- A hoax video of a pig rescuing a goat reached seven million views; crew were under NDAs; revealed Feb 2013. [secondary]

## Career decisions

- **Business to comedy.** Studied science at UBC, then Bachelor of Commerce at UVic (2005), worked briefly at a BC brokerage, moved to Toronto 2005 to try comedy, Humber comedy program 2006. [secondary: Wikipedia] His persona is a business-school grad offering consulting - the original career is the joke's engine. [inference]
- **CBC segment.** Correspondent on *This Hour Has 22 Minutes* with "Nathan on Your Side" (2008-09). Nathan for You grew out of it with writer Michael Koman. Greenlit after *Jon Benjamin Has a Van* was cancelled. [secondary]
- **Nathan for You to the end.** The network said in Oct 2018 that Fielder chose to end the show and move on; no public reason from him was found, though commentators judged "Finding Frances" a deliberate ending. [secondary; the "deliberate" reading is [inference]]
- **Between shows.** Consulting producer/director on *Who Is America?* (2018); HBO overall deal 2019; executive produced *How To with John Wilson*. [secondary]
- **The Rehearsal S1 (2022).** Aired from 15 Jul 2022; renewed Aug 2022. Season ends with Nathan in a mother role telling the child actor "I'm your dad" - a deliberately ambiguous breakdown/breakthrough, after earlier sequences considering whether he should have used child actors at all. Several participants (Angela, Robbin, Patrick) left their rehearsals. [secondary: AV Club, Wikipedia] Critics split on whether the finale is genius or exploitative (Rolling Stone, Flicks, New Yorker's Brody). [secondary]
- **The Curse (Showtime, 2023-24).** Co-created with Benny Safdie; Fielder and Safdie say they tried to talk themselves out of making it, but each doubt produced more ideas; premise born from a street encounter where a woman "cursed" Fielder and he felt relieved after giving her $20; Safdie added the HGTV-style renovation-reality angle. They mapped the whole story before pitching and shot in Espanola, New Mexico, using many local non-actors and working with community officials. [secondary: Yahoo/other] Same method: take a small real emotional residue and build a world to test it.
- **Rehearsal S2 and aviation (2025).** Per his Jeff Wise interview (New York, May 2025): he started flight training around January 2023, before pitching HBO; he had never wanted to be a pilot; instructors treated him as unusually slow; he found CRM training often a 20-minute PowerPoint; he would testify before Congress if asked; he said the show forces him to "genuinely dig myself out of a hole." [primary via secondary reproduction; quote not independently verified]. The season includes a real meeting with Rep. Steve Cohen, who was not convinced and said such training should be left to airlines. He then did a ferry-pilot job. [secondary]
- **The 737 flight.** Roughly 280 flight hours (airlines typically require 1,500). He took type-rating training in Nevada, filled a 737 with actors as passengers so the "no paying passengers" rule was respected, and flew about 2h10 from San Bernardino. [secondary: EW/AOL, InsideHook] He deleted a doctor's voicemail about fMRI results after reading Reddit warnings that disclosing a diagnosis can cost licences, then said this mirrored pilots' silence about mental health. [secondary]
- **Awards.** Two Emmy nominations (directing, writing, "Pilot's Code") in 2025. [secondary: Wikipedia]
- **2025-2026.** HBO CEO Casey Bloys said in Sept 2025 Fielder "has an idea" for more; reporting says he is still developing Season 3, no date. [secondary] *You Can See Everything*, co-directed with Lance Oppenheim, is a roughly three-hour Elizabeth Holmes documentary; Holmes invited a crew to document her before prison; Fielder planned to stay behind the camera, then moved in two weeks before her report date and became a central figure; says he gave them many chances to turn cameras off; premiered at Telluride (Sept 2026) and opens theatrically via A24 on 16 Oct 2026. At the Q&A he said he still struggles to understand what he experienced. [secondary: Boston.com, Telluride coverage]

## Handling fallout

- **Dumb Starbucks (2014).** Health-department closure absorbed by the plan: reveal, press conference, direct customers to the real owner's cafe. [secondary]
- **Summit Ice removal (2023-25).** Instead of a press release, he made it an on-screen set piece in The Rehearsal S2 and called it "proudest achievement." Paramount said "standards review"; reportedly had not expected the on-air attack (AV Club). [secondary]
- **FAA rebuttal (May 2025).** The FAA said it already requires interpersonal-communication training and sees no data that supports his central claim. Fielder on CNN: "That's dumb" and "They're dumb." [primary quote via CJR/AV Club]. He also said he trained as a pilot and is a 737 pilot, claimed his flight was the "Miracle over the Mojave," and referenced communication issues in the Potomac crash. He later posted on social media saying John Goglia had advised a different tone (per BroBible, low-reliability). [secondary]
- **Participant ethics (Rehearsal S1).** Critics (New Yorker's Richard Brody) called the gaze cruel and arrogant; recurring concerns about covert data collection and emotional toll on Remy and Patrick. No direct Fielder apology found; the finale itself stages his self-questioning and a final act of apologising to the child actor in character. [secondary]
- **Aviation experts.** Journalist Jeff Wise started skeptical and concluded after talking to experts that Fielder had identified a real issue, though CJR remained doubtful it merits a full season given flying is very safe. [secondary]

## Say/do consistency

- Consistent: he said he would testify to Congress and then staged a congressional-hearing rehearsal plus a real meeting with a congressman; he said he wanted better communication training and then trained himself and flew a 737. [secondary]
- Consistent: the loophole logic is stated openly in-show (actors as passengers) rather than hidden. [secondary]
- Mixed: he claimed the S2 mission was serious safety reform, yet frames the finale as a stunt ("Miracle over the Mojave"). [secondary, see Contradictions]
- Consistent on Summit Ice: the 2015 charitable gesture and the 2025 defence are aligned.
- Persona vs. person: the in-show Nathan claims business motives (Dumb Starbucks "get rich") while real outcomes are mostly artistic/charitable. [inference]

## Contradictions

1. **Serious reform vs. comedy.** He says the FAA/pilot-communication work is genuine and would testify, yet the FAA found no supporting data and he answered with insults on CNN; CJR notes the safety premise is contested. Both claims coexist. [secondary]
2. **Pilot-hour norm vs. loophole.** He argues pilots need better human training, but his own flight succeeded because he used a loophole that bypasses the 1,500-hour norm. Press noted he was not qualified for an airline job. [secondary]
3. **Health disclosure.** He criticises pilots for hiding mental-health issues while deliberately avoiding his own fMRI results. He frames this as symmetry; critics may read it as the very behaviour he criticised. [secondary + inference]
4. **Summit Ice fundraising.** Wikipedia ($150,000+ by 2017), Fielder (millions, self-reported) and a UK outlet (more than GBP 240,000) disagree; none verified here. [secondary]
5. **Summit Ice discovery date.** late 2022 vs late 2023 in different reports. [secondary]
6. **Autonomy vs. "ended by network."** Comedy Central says Fielder chose to end Nathan for You; no direct quote from him found; some press framed it as cancellation. [secondary]
7. **Finale ambiguity.** Whether the Finding Frances and Rehearsal S1 endings are sincere or staged is deliberately unresolved by the makers; critics disagree. [secondary]
